FIPS Lab

A chaos lab for FIPS, a network in which every machine is identified by its own key, so two machines can find and trust each other without DNS or a certificate authority in between. The lab runs it in Docker, breaks it on purpose, and measures how it heals. 111 measured cuts turned up a five-minute window in which traffic can get stuck in a routing loop, which a Bayesian model then pinned down from the outside.

50 SIMULATED WEEKS · MONEY FRONTED

Payments · Simulation

G2C

An escrow for digital goods that charges the buyer and pays the seller as one step: the goods and the money move together, or neither moves. Payments run over the Lightning Network, which can hold a payment until the trade is done; in-game items are the first use. I ran its real state machine through 50 simulated weeks of failures to find out what running it takes: the money it pays out before it collects, the fee it needs and when to alert an operator.

About

I would rather make things than consume them. That can be software or something physical, and often it means taking something that already exists and improving it.

Much of what I build is about removing third parties. The internet relies on a few central parties for identity: DNS maps names to addresses, and certificate authorities vouch for who is on the other end of a connection. When one of them makes a mistake, everyone who depends on it is exposed. FIPS, the mesh network in my first project, works differently. Every node is identified by its own key, so two machines can find and trust each other without a company like Tailscale in between.

I prefer local-first software for the same reason. Files on my own laptop, my own database and my own servers keep working whatever happens to a provider. The price is responsibility. If I break something, there is no support desk to call and no one else's backup to fall back on, and I am happy to make that trade.

I have worked as a data scientist and as a software developer, and I test these systems the way I would test a statistical model, with experiments and with predictions that could turn out to be wrong.
