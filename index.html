FIPS Lab

A chaos lab for FIPS, a network in which every machine is identified by its own key, so two machines can find and trust each other without DNS or a certificate authority in between. The lab runs it in Docker, breaks it on purpose, and measures how it heals. 111 measured cuts turned up a five-minute window in which traffic can get stuck in a routing loop, which a Bayesian model then pinned down from the outside.

7 / 10cuts stuck when made 90 s after start-up
301 ± 1 shidden timer, inferred from measurements alone
5 / 5unseen test cuts inside the predicted range

Payments · Simulation

G2C

An escrow for digital goods that charges the buyer and pays the seller as one step: the goods and the money move together, or neither moves. Payments run over the Lightning Network, which can hold a payment until the trade is done; in-game items are the first use. I ran its real state machine through 50 simulated weeks of failures to find out what running it takes: the money it pays out before it collects, the fee it needs and when to alert an operator.

About

I am a data scientist and software developer, and I like to own a problem from the first question to the working system. I build the software myself, and then I check with data whether it does what it should.

Most of that work is now with AI. For Shared Board I measured how the design of a tool changes the work of three Claude models, over 414 graded runs, with a Bayesian model that separates the effect of the tool from the difficulty of the task. This is the work I want to do more of: finding out how well an AI system works and what it costs, before a product depends on it.

I prefer probabilities over point estimates. A result with an honest range tells a team how much risk it is taking, and a model that makes predictions can be checked. In FIPS Lab, a model inferred a hidden timer of 301 ± 1 s from outside measurements alone, against 300 s in the source code, and I committed its predictions for new runs before I made them.

I would rather make things than only use them, and I like software that keeps working without depending on a provider. That comes with responsibility, since there is no support desk to call, and it has made me careful about what happens when things fail. Before running a payment service for real money, I put its own code through 50 simulated weeks of outages and lost messages.
